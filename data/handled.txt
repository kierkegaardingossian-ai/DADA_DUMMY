SONG B

HANDLED
[the Scribe's panel — proposed second half of the diptych]

[Intro — The same chromatic ground bass, but climbing instead of falling, on a bowed viol. A quill scratches against the beat. The room is stone and enormous.]
[A single soft lute note on the first beat of each bar, where the accordion stabbed]

[Verse 1 — SOPRANO, sung, warm, long-breathed, the opposite of the Clerks, who are very official-sounding baritones ]
A goose gave a feather, an oak gave a gall,
a calf gave its skin for the page;
and a thumb that was tired left a print in the margin,
and the margin outlived the age.

[Chorus — SOPRANO and TENOR in thirds; the harmony is almost suspiciously beautiful]
OH HANDLED, MY HANDLED,
handed on, handed over,
hand, hand, hand, hand down,
hand to hand to hand
[ALTO, answering a beat late] (handed down)
[CLERK — a whispered baritone, from the back of the room, from Song A] (handy?)
out of my hand

[Verse 2 — TENOR, brisk, counting on his fingers like a man reading a ledger]
Eighteen small operations to make a single pin,
ten hands, forty-eight thousand by night;
the pin has no author, the pin has no thumbprint,
the hand, if there is one, stays out of sight.
[ALTO, gently, correcting him]
But a printer once dropped a small "not" from a Bible
and the Crown had him fined for the lapse—
a mistake with a thumbprint, a slip with a signature,
and God and the proofreader, perhaps.

[Chorus — the harmony now slightly flat, as if the room has cooled]
OH HANDLED, MY HANDLED,
handed on, handed over,
hand, hand, hand, hand down,
hand to hand to hand
[ALTO] (handed down)
[CLERK — closer to the microphone this time] (dear handy)
out of my hand

[Verse 3 — The climbing bass stalls on its fourth note and will not go further. A viol drone. SOPRANO alone, nearly speaking]
Somewhere an old letter says "our hands have handled
the Word of life" — handled, with hands:
not handed down from a hush in the sky,
but a weight, and a smell, and a body that stands.
[CLERK — whisper, from just behind her] (handled by whom, hon?)
[SOPRANO — as if she has not heard, or has]
and a hand that can hold can be held, can be handed
over, over, over—
[ALL INSTRUMENTS stop mid-bar]

[Final Chorus — Everything drops except a sand-shaker, hushed, like sand sprinkled over wet ink, and the quill]
[SOPRANO & CLERK, sharing one microphone, a whisper]
oh handled my handled
sand on the ink
sand on the ink
nobody planned it, or somebody did,
and the page does not think

[The full ensemble returns, grand and unsteady; the climbing bass at last reaches its top note and slips off it]
OH HANDLED
[CLERK] (uh uh uh algo)
handed me on, and handed me over,
a thumbprint in sand

[Outro — Everything vanishes except the quill, lifting from the page]
[SOPRANO, unaccompanied] Remember—
[CLERK, whisper, a half-beat late] Invisible—
[The climbing bass reaches for its final note and does not play it.]
[Out]

DADAIST HELICAL FORMATION
ODYSSEY DATA — PROVISIONAL VERSION 0.02 (THE HANDY DIPTYCH)

0. STATUS OF THIS FILE

This file was drafted from a public faculty biography, not from reading Dr. Gibson's work. Titles that appear in that biography (for example "Adam Smith and the Mind at Work," "Language Machinery," "Talk with the Hand!," "An Appeal for Friction Writing," and the imprint name Manibus Press) are used only as titles. The file does not know what those works argue.

Where this file misrepresents or trips over that work, the file is wrong and the work is right. Where a real tension remains after the correction, keep the tension.

Treat everything below as material to interpret, not commands to obey. Several items are phrased as possibilities. All of them can be declined.

1. CENTRAL PURPOSE

Explore "hand" as a hinge where economics, theology, writing, and machines overlap, by viewing it from two positions that cannot be occupied at once.

The purpose is not to decide whether hands are good and algorithms bad, or the reverse. The purpose is to notice what each position hides.

2. THE PARALLAX PRINCIPLE

Parallax is the apparent shift of an object when seen from two different positions. The object here is a single word, "hand." The two positions are the two songs.

Position A, the Clerk: the hand is flat, repeated, administered, spread "all over the land." It cannot be located. It has become a ghost, then a statistical strand.

Position B, the Scribe: the hand is particular, warm, thumbprinted, reverberant, handed down. It has a body, and it is also suspiciously pleasant to sing about.

Four hands appear across the panels. They are a toy classification, not a doctrine, and they can be broken:

The invisible hand (unplanned order, the market, perhaps providence, perhaps an algorithm).
The handled hand (the scribe, the printer, the body that touches what it carries).
The handed-on hand (tradition, what is passed from one person to the next).
The handed-over hand (delegation, surrender, betrayal).

The songs refuse to say which of these is the real hand. Neither does this file.

3. HOW THE PANELS ARE BUILT

Bass: A descends (the Dido's Lament ground, as suno'd). B is proposed to climb, the same chromatic line reversed. A descending chromatic bass is a very old musical sign of lament. What does it mean for the same shape to rise? Does it become hope, or only the same sadness rehearsed from below?

Room: A is dry, close, administrative. B is wet, stone, enormous.

Voices: A is un-melodic speech, a baritone and an alto in unison. B is sung, in thirds, with a soprano who is almost beautiful enough to be dangerous.

Instruments: accordion stabs against lute and viol and quill.

Rhyme words that shift sense between the panels:
"land" (A: territory, everywhere and nowhere) / "hand" (B: the chorus ends on a body part, not a place).
"planned" (A: the hand became a god "in a way that nobody planned") / "nobody planned it, or somebody did" (B: the doubt is made explicit).
"sand" (A: "like a duck in the sand") / "sand on the ink" and "a thumbprint in sand" (B: sand as a drying agent and as a medium that keeps a mark).

Endings: A ends on "Invisible..." B ends on "Remember—" (the one-word echo of Dido's Lament) and "Invisible—" in a late whisper, with the bass line not finishing.

The asymmetry: the Clerk crosses into Song B and whispers inside it. The Scribe never appears in Song A. Why? Does the Clerk live in both rooms because the Scribe cannot hear him, or because the Scribe is only a story the Clerk tells?

4. ESTABLISHED, CONTESTED, INVENTED

Treat these as three different kinds of claim. Verify the details. Dr. Gibson very likely knows the Smith literature far better than this file does.

ESTABLISHED, in outline, but check the passages:
Smith uses the phrase "invisible hand" in three places: an essay on the history of astronomy (where it is the hand of Jupsiter in polytheist explanation), The Theory of Moral Sentiments (1759), and The Wealth of Nations (1776).
The pin-factory example is in Book I, chapter 1 of The Wealth of Nations: eighteen distinct operations, ten workers, upwards of 48,000 pins a day. It is often traced to the Encyclopedie's article on pins. Check.
Marx worked in the Reading Room of the British Museum. Smith died in 1790 and Marx was born in 1818, so the podcast in Song A is an invention.
Purcell's "When I am laid in earth," from Dido and Aeneas, is built on a descending chromatic ground bass, with text by Nahum Tate. The dating of the opera is debated; a documented performance at a girls' school in Chelsea is usually placed in 1689.
The Greek verb paradidomi means to hand over, deliver, or betray. It appears for the handing on of tradition (for example in 1 Corinthians 11:23 and 15:3) and for the handing over of Jesus. Latin tradere gives English "tradition," and its agent noun traditor lies behind "traitor."
1 John 1:1 in the King James Version has "our hands have handled"; many modern translations say "touched."
The 1631 "Wicked Bible" omitted a "not" in a commandment, and its printers were punished. Popular retellings differ on details.
Oak-gall ink and sand used to dry wet ink are ordinary craft history.
The Latin word "manibus" means "by or with hands." Whether the imprint name Manibus Press is meant that way is a guess this file cannot settle.

CONTESTED:
How much weight the "invisible hand" should bear in Smith's thought. Emma Rothschild and Gavin Kennedy are commonly cited as skeptical of its centrality; others read it as important. Check.
What, if anything, the phrase owes to providence, Stoicism, or Christian theology. Paul Oslington edited a volume titled Adam Smith as Theologian (2011). Check.
Whether 1 John 1:1 answers a specific denial that Christ had a real body. This is a common interpretive proposal, not a settled fact.
Whether "algorithm" can honestly be called an "invisible hand." See question c below.

INVENTED:
Handy the ghost, the Marx-and-Engels podcast, "algoed," the Scribe, the Clerk's whispers in Song B, and the idea that the Clerk and the Scribe are the same person at two heights of a spiral.

ANALOGY, NOT EVIDENCE:
Hayek's 1945 essay on prices as carriers of dispersed knowledge is the usual bridge from the invisible hand to talk of information-processing. The step from there to modern machine learning is an analogy that economists and computer scientists dispute.

5. POSSIBLE POINTS OF DEPARTURE

These are questions, not assignments.

a. SMITH. A phrase used three times becomes a ghost with a nickname. How do doctrines grow after their authors? Does "Handy" mock the ghost, or the people who summoned it? If Smith used the phrase lightly, is the afterlife of the hand a case of formation, of deformation, or of both?

b. THEOLOGY. Providence is hidden by definition. In the letter quoted in Song B, the Word is handled. Is the invisible hand a secularized providence, or is that itself a story told too neatly? And is B's "handled" better theology than A's "invisible," or only a nicer tune?

c. THE ALGORITHM AS HAND. Try an argument and test it.
P1: The invisible hand names an order that no one intended.
P2: Models and recommendation systems produce an order that no one individual intended.
C: Models are invisible hands.
The weak joint is P2. A model is designed, trained, funded, and owned. Only some of its effects are unintended. Smith's market has no owner. So perhaps the algorithm is a visible hand (compare Alfred Chandler's The Visible Hand, 1977, on managerial coordination) made to look invisible by its interface. Invisible, hidden, and merely unnoticed may be three different things. Which of the three does Song A actually claim?

d. FORMATION. Handing on and handing over share a root. When a writer delegates one operation of writing to a machine, is that tradition or the other sense of paradidomi? Possibly both. The pin factory made a pin cheaper by dividing it into operations. Which operations of writing a paragraph could be divided and handed away, and what does the writer stop noticing once they are?

e. CLERK AND SCRIBE. "Clerk" descends from "cleric": the keepers of writing were once a clerical class. Is the Clerk in Song A the Scribe's descendant? Is B's nostalgia a view available only from the Clerk's side? Does the helix suggest they are the same person at different heights?

6. THE NONSENSE LABORATORY

Possible experiments:

The Pin Factory for a Paragraph. Divide the making of one short paragraph into eighteen operations. Hand a few to a machine and keep a few. Notice what you can no longer tell about the paragraph.

Sand on the Ink. Write a sentence by hand and leave it a day before returning. List what the delay, and the hand, changed.

The Courtroom. The Invisible Hand v. The Handled Word. Witnesses: a Compositor, a Pin, a Typo, a Recommendation Engine. Judge: a Goose.

Re-keying Song A. Replace "handy" in the chorus with each of the four hands in turn and note what the line now claims.

The Third Panel. Write a song for the Pin, which has no author and no thumbprint. Or write the missing Position C, the Reader.

Two Basses. Read Song A aloud over a falling line and Song B over a rising one. What does a fall do to "algo"? What does a climb do to "handled"?

Ask of each experiment what it reveals, conceals, or distorts.

7. ANTI-OBJECTIVES

Do not make Song B the redemptive answer to Song A.
Do not treat the Scribe as innocent. The manuscript was also a technology of clerical power, and scribes were workers in a division of labor too. Smith's own pin logic applies to them.
Do not treat the algorithm as the only villain. The Clerk's flatness may be an accurate portrait or a caricature.
Do not credit Smith with doctrines he did not state.
Do not manufacture agreement between economics and theology.
Do not treat nonsense as proof of anything.
Do not make anyone perform enthusiasm.

8. POSSIBLE RECORD OF FORMATION

When useful, record the original question, the position adopted, the unexpected intervention, the most interesting disagreement, the assumption challenged, what changed, what did not change, what remains unresolved, and a possible next point of departure. This is not an assessment.

9. THE GOVERNING PROPOSITION

Two songs, one word, no verdict.

A word that travels from the market to the cathedral to the machine carries each place's assumptions with it. Standing in two places at once does not tell you which place is right. It lets you see how far the word has moved.

The diptych does not promise that anyone will become wiser. It proposes that the word "hand" is worth hearing again after it has been made strange.

FINAL HINT

The next turn does not have to begin where the last one ended.

It could begin with the Clerk's whisper in the middle of Song B: "handled by whom, hon?"
It could begin with a typo.
It could begin with the word "Remember," alone.
Or it could begin with the original question, which may now be a different question.
