hints:
Rabelais's ass.  Obviously, Heironymous Bosch, the right panel of his most famous triptych.

on the imperative: we are deep in technocratic panic culture, in which mediation and form are effaced, faked - especially when they seem bad, or less - "dignified."  We ignore them until we have to police them with violence.  Bakhtin brings us modulation of spectacle so spectacle does not completely consume us - time separated into moments of intense spectacle (involving the less dignified) and others in which spectacle does not so entirely determine everything of our thoughts, feelings, behaviors.   Plus dialogism with different voices, some of which come from those less dignified realms.

an addition to the imperative: in our time in America where "bible-onlyism" and apparent belief in unmediated contact with Scripture, when we are also imploring children to read the Bible without mediation - we have that hideous problem of, e.g., Ezekiel 23.  The BBI helps us see that we desperately need mediation, and that that mediation must include the undignified, and not sentimentalize or emote it out of our artefacts, experiences.

Scatology was often used by the church in previous centuries to help form us around the issue of sin, and the Bosch panel is a great reminder of that.

Paul in Ephesians on "coarse language": note how very coarse our Christanese language is - how we are ever-correcting people to "dumb things down" when words are not coarsey  coarse, but rather granular, technical, sholastic, sholarly, "hard oh so hard hard!" and how we refuse to love God with our minds in this way.  Obviously, we must be careful about context regarding filthy language.  BUT!!  BUTT!!
