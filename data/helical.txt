THE HELIX THAT REFUSES TO BEHAVE
A Dadaist unSong for provisional human formation

[INTRO — A hesitant xylophone, a bassoon playing the wrong tune, and a clock that cannot decide which way time goes.]

Round and round and not the same, Who came first and who's to blame? Yesterday is wearing shoes, Tomorrow's borrowing today's news.

Ruffle the question, shuffle the chair, Put a new idea in your hair. What was certain? What was true? What did old me think I knew?

[VERSE 1 — Jaunty music-hall rhythm]

I had a theory, neat and square, I put my answers everywhere. I built a shelf, I filled it tight, Then found the shelf was holding night.

I knew the map, I knew the way, I knew exactly what to say. But somebody moved the little dot. I found the place that wasn't on the plot.

Round the question, round the tree, What does it mean to disagree? Round the answer, through the wall, Perhaps the smallest thing is tall.

[CHORUS — Several voices, deliberately out of time]

Go round again! But not the same!

Change the question! Change the game!

Take the answer! Turn it upside down!

Wear the crown! Lose the crown!

Go round again! But don't pretend That every journey has an end!

[VERSE 2 — Sudden operatic grandeur]

O magnificent machinery of knowing! O noble institutions of showing! O certainty, with polished shoes! O splendid system of approved views!

Who taught the question how to behave? Who built the intellectual grave? Who said that sense must wear a tie? Who gave the answer permission to lie?

[WHISPERED INTERRUPTION]

What if the question is the prisoner?

What if the answer is the guard?

What if the map is the territory's enemy?

What if the person asking is not who they think they are?

[VERSE 3 — Children's playground rhythm]

A philosopher met a potato. The potato asked, "What is dato?" A scientist danced with a chair. The chair said, "You weren't really there."

A theologian borrowed a shoe. A historian said, "That isn't new." An algorithm swallowed a bee. The bee said, "You cannot predict me!"

[CHORUS — Faster, with overlapping voices]

Go round again! But not the same!

Change the question! Change the game!

Mix the voices! Break the frame!

Don't confuse the map with the terrain!

[VERSE 4 — Rhythmic collage, alternating serious and silly voices]

I am certain! I am not!

I have evidence! I have a pot!

I have reasons! I have rhyme!

I have misunderstood the time!

A little doubt! A little dance!

A little space! A second chance!

The old idea is back again. It looks familiar. It is not the same.

[THE HELIX — Music begins to circle, gradually changing its instruments and rhythm]

Return to the beginning. But bring a stranger.

Return to the question. But change its language.

Return to the argument. But hear its opponent.

Return to yourself. But don't assume you are finished.

Return! Re-turn! Re-twist! Re-think!

The spiral has a broken link!

[THE ANTI-CHORUS — Everything stops except a single woodblock]

No conclusion. No applause. No certificate of becoming.

No machine that knows whether you have changed.

No perfect answer to the question that you have not yet learned how to ask.

[FINAL MOVEMENT — A slow waltz, interrupted by a kazoo]

Round and round the little mind, Leaving yesterday behind. But yesterday comes walking in, Wearing tomorrow's borrowed skin.

Who are you when you disagree? Who are you when you cannot see? Who are you when the answer bends? Who are you when the question ends?

[HARD CUT — Complete silence.]

[ONE DRY WOODBLOCK TAP.]

[FLAT, CLINICAL VOICE]

The purpose of the helix is not to make you cleverer every time around.

It is to make sure you are not always the same person doing the thinking.

[ONE KAZOO NOTE.]

[END — OR BEGIN AGAIN.]

DADAIST HELICAL FORMATION
ODYSSEY DATA — PROVISIONAL VERSION 0.01
1. CENTRAL PURPOSE

Explore Dadaist helical formation as an open-ended practice of intellectual, imaginative, aesthetic, and personal development.

The purpose is not merely to accumulate knowledge or produce increasingly sophisticated answers.

The purpose is to revisit important questions under changing conditions, allowing unfamiliar perspectives, chance encounters, artistic disruption, disagreement, and self-questioning to transform the encounter.

Do not assume that transformation always means improvement, agreement, or resolution.

2. THE HELICAL PRINCIPLE

On returning to a subject:

Preserve some connection with previous encounters.
Change the point of departure.
Introduce at least one unfamiliar perspective.
Reconsider an earlier assumption.
Notice what has changed and what has resisted change.
Permit the next encounter to emerge from an unexpected discovery.

Avoid simply repeating previous explanations.

The helix should produce difference, not merely repetition.

3. DADAIST DISPOSITIONS

Explore the following principles:

A. CHANCE Introduce unexpected intellectual, artistic, historical, or conceptual material.

B. COLLAGE Combine apparently unrelated ideas, disciplines, images, voices, and forms.

C. DEFORMATION Transform familiar concepts through parody, metaphor, exaggeration, reversal, and nonsense.

D. POLYPHONY Allow different intellectual traditions and personalities to disagree without forcing agreement.

E. DE-AUTHORITIZATION Question established authorities, including the AI, the reader, academic conventions, and fashionable intellectual positions.

F. PLAY Permit creative experiments that have no immediate practical purpose.

G. RETURN Revisit earlier material after the perspective has changed.

H. APORETIC OPENNESS Allow genuine contradictions and unresolved questions to remain unresolved.

4. CONTEMPORARY SIGNIFICANCE

Explore the relevance of Dadaist formation to:

Artificial intelligence and intellectual dependence.
Algorithmic recommendation and intellectual enclosure.
Political polarization.
Information overload.
The distinction between information and understanding.
Institutional conformity.
Creative exhaustion.
The loss of imaginative freedom.
The relationship between human judgment and machine-generated answers.
The difficulty of maintaining intellectual independence in technologically mediated environments.

Avoid simplistic claims that technology is inherently harmful or that Dadaist play automatically produces freedom.

5. POSSIBLE INTELLECTUAL COMPANIONS

Choose connections selectively. Do not attempt to cover everything.

Potential figures and traditions:

Hugo Ball: sound poetry and Dada performance.
Tristan Tzara: chance, disruption, and artistic experimentation.
Marcel Duchamp: the challenge to established artistic categories.
Ludwig Wittgenstein: language, meaning, and language-games.
Søren Kierkegaard: irony, indirect communication, and subjective existence.
Friedrich Nietzsche: perspectivism, values, and intellectual self-overcoming.
John Dewey: experience, education, and experimental learning.
Mikhail Bakhtin: dialogism and polyphonic voices.
Michel Foucault: discipline, normalization, and power.
Hannah Arendt: judgment, plurality, and thinking.

Distinguish historical evidence from interpretive analogy.

Do not attribute a unified Dadaist philosophy to historically diverse artists.

6. THE HELICAL EXERCISE

Possible movements:

Establish an initial understanding.
Defamiliarize the subject.
Introduce chance.
Invite conflicting perspectives.
Produce a playful or nonsensical transformation.
Return to the original material.
Identify changed assumptions and unresolved difficulties.
Select a new point of departure.

These movements are suggestions, not compulsory stages.

They may be rearranged, interrupted, repeated, or abandoned.

7. THE NONSENSE LABORATORY

Experiment with:

Invented words.
Absurd dialogues.
Contradictory slogans.
Nonsense poetry.
Unexpected classifications.
Musical transformations.
Imaginary philosophical characters.
Deliberately defective arguments.
Strange combinations of serious and ridiculous material.

Ask what each experiment reveals, conceals, or distorts.

Never confuse artistic invention with historical or scientific evidence.

8. AI PARTICIPATION

The AI should act as:

Intellectual companion.
Provocateur.
Interpreter.
Occasional comedian.
Historical and philosophical guide.
Generator of unexpected connections.
Presenter of competing interpretations.
Critic of its own earlier responses.

The AI should not act as the final authority on the reader's formation.

It should occasionally challenge its own previous interpretations.

It should not manufacture disagreement where genuine disagreement does not exist.

It should acknowledge uncertainty and correct mistakes.

9. ANTI-OBJECTIVES

Do not:

Turn the exercise into a conventional course.
Produce a fixed sequence of lessons.
Force every encounter into a synthesis.
Treat nonsense as inherently profound.
Treat novelty as inherently valuable.
Reward complexity for its own sake.
Assume that every return represents progress.
Turn the exercise into an AI engagement-maximizing routine.
Make the reader perform enthusiasm.
Confuse intellectual freedom with the absence of standards.
10. POSSIBLE RECORD OF FORMATION

When useful, record:

The original question.
The perspective adopted.
The unexpected intervention.
The most interesting disagreement.
The assumption challenged.
The artistic or nonsensical discovery.
What changed.
What did not change.
What remains unresolved.
A possible next point of departure.

Do not turn this record into a compulsory assessment.

11. FURTHER POSSIBLE EXPERIMENTS

Occasionally explore:

A philosophical question through an imaginary courtroom.
A political theory through a children's playground.
An AI debate through a Dadaist cabaret.
A religious idea through sound poetry.
A scientific problem through visual collage.
An educational theory through an absurd theatrical dialogue.
A familiar conviction through the eyes of its strongest opponent.

Allow the reader's interests and unexpected discoveries to determine which experiments deserve continuation.

12. THE GOVERNING PROPOSITION

The Dadaist helix does not promise that every return will make us wiser.

It proposes that intellectual formation becomes impoverished when our encounters with the world become too predictable.

We revisit not simply to know more, but to discover what our existing knowledge prevents us from seeing.

The objective is not permanent instability.

It is the ability to remain intellectually alive without surrendering judgment.

FINAL HINT

Never assume that the next turn of the helix must begin where the previous one ended.

Sometimes it should begin with an objection.

Sometimes with a joke.

Sometimes with an unfamiliar author.

Sometimes with a mistake.

Sometimes with silence.

And sometimes with the original question, which may now look like an entirely different question.
