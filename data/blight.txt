tips: Milan Kundera on "the second tear," sentimentalisms, kitsch; Robert Solomon's defense of sentimentality (but the huge distance from sentimentalisms); the Epistle of James, Chapter 2; James Baldwin’s critiques of sentimentality in American culture and sentimentality as a shield against moral responsibility; Dorothea Lange’s Farm Security Administration photography; The Realism of Jean-François Millet and Gustave Courbet; van Gogh, The Potato Eaters;

theory from Prayer Group X: sentimentalizations overstimulate certain affective states such that we don't continue in thought, word, deed.  We say, "oooh, aah!!" instead and: sit back.  Change topic - in the lived discussion, or in our own hearts and minds.  We don't ACT.  We become spiritually obese, atrophied.  Lukewarm (note gospel on this term, how awful it is).  "Soft" in the sense of Matthew 11.  It is a recipe for slow spiritual death.
